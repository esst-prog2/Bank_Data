Notes to the plate

1

Every figure says where it came from.

The figures come from three kinds of public, machine-readable source: banks' own ESEF annual filings, the EBA Pillar 3 Data Hub (templates EU KM1, OV1, CQ3 and LI1), and the EBA EU-wide Stress Test. Each value is mapped to a standardized concept and tagged with its source and provenance: a reported actual, or a scenario-projected stress-test figure. The two are never mixed.

Data that is missing from the source is reported as missing, with the reason. A figure the package failed to retrieve is reported differently, so a gap in a filing never looks like a bug, and a bug never looks like a gap.

2

Where two filings disagree, both stay on the page.

Total risk exposure is published twice in Pillar 3: once in EU KM1 and once in EU OV1. For two pilot banks the totals don't match. The package doesn't pick one or average them. It returns both, each tagged by its template.

Amber marks a disagreement here and in the dashboard alike. It isn't an error: it's the package showing both sources instead of hiding the gap. In the dashboard, hover an amber cell to see each figure with its template; these cells are left out of best-value highlighting, because there is no single number to rank.

3

How to try it: three steps, no install.

The dashboard is one static page. Every number in it was precomputed by calling the package's own functions, so nothing runs on a server and nothing is simplified for display.

  1. Build a basket on the map.

    Open the dashboard and click bank logos. Up to six go into the basket at the bottom.

  2. Choose what to compare.

    Press Compare and tick concepts across capital, balance sheet, profitability, RWA breakdown, liquidity and asset quality. Many come as a ratio to total assets or to total RWA.

  3. Read the table, then export it.

    The best value per ratio row is highlighted. A dash means the bank doesn't report that figure this way; hover it for the reason. Download CSV saves a timestamped copy.

4

Under the plate: a Python package.

Given a bank's LEI, a reporting period and a list of concepts, get_financial_data() returns a pandas DataFrame with one row per figure and source, carrying its source reporting item, standardized concept and provenance. The dashboard calls these four functions as shipped:

  • get_financial_data()figures, sources and provenance
  • compute_rwa_density()RWA over total assets
  • compute_cost_to_income_ratio()operating expenses over income
  • compute_npl_ratio()non-performing over total exposures

5

What this plate covers, and what it doesn't.

This is a course-term MVP. The boundaries are deliberate and stated up front.

Covered this term

  • 28 banks in 13 countries, with data available through ESEF
  • Capital, RWA, balance sheet, profitability, liquidity and asset-quality concepts
  • Source and provenance on every figure
  • Missing data distinguished from retrieval failures

Not this term

  • Every European bank, or banks outside ESEF coverage
  • German banks: Germany's ESEF filings aren't available through the aggregator used, and its register blocks automated access
  • A public API or hosted service
  • Reconciling different accounting frameworks into one comparable number
